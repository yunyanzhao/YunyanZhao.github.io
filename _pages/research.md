Current project
------
My doctoral dissertation investigates youth social media experiences and their implications for psychological well-being. Integrating perspectives from developmental science, family studies, sociology, and human-computer interaction (HCI), and in collaboration with Matthew Coetzee and Adnan Hoq, this project examines how everyday digital experiences are embedded within broader relational, technological, and cultural contexts.

Specifically, I use a multi-method approach, including surveys, ecological momentary assessment (EMA), and in-depth interviews, to capture both between-person differences and within-person dynamics in youth social media use. This design allows me to examine how momentary experiences (e.g., social interaction, digital use patterns) are associated with fluctuations in affect and well-being in daily life, as well as how these processes vary across relational and cultural contexts among diverse populations. In addition, qualitative interviews provide insight into youth subjective interpretations of their offline and online experiences, enriching and contextualizing quantitative findings.

This project has been supported by the [Graduate Student Research Award (GSRA)](https://franco.nd.edu/for-students/funding/graduate-student-research-award-gsra/), and I have also pursued additional funding through the [Initiative on Race and Resilience (IRR)](https://raceandresilience.nd.edu/funding/research-grants/) at the University of Notre Dame.
